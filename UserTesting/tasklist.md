| Capture     | You are preparing for your algorithms exam, and know that you'll need to be able to recall some of the key ideas behind mergesort's proof of correctness. Walk me through how you would make some quick notes.|Capturing points is the first step of the app's workflow, and the context provided allows me to check if the connection between the app's workflow and its intended use case (studying for technical subjects) is clear from the user interface. In a cognitive breakdown, I may not note for myself how stringent the name length restriction is for points. A user can express frustrations or verbalize whether it feels like they can make a meaningful point with the given character limit.|
| Organization | You remember that your exam will also involve reasoning about the correctness and runtimes of insertion sort, quicksort, and heapsort. Walk me through how you would make notes for those, as well as how you would organize them.| Organizing points into topics is the next step in the general workflow, and testing for it also allows me to see how easily users can discover features like the click and drag to put multiple points into the same topic and the move to topic button in the editor. In my cognitive walkthrough, I might not have an accurate understanding of how annoying it feels to reorganize points and topics, and a user can highlight those annoyances if they come up.|
| Search     | You have created a variety of points and topics regarding algorithms, and now you would like to edit one of them. Show me how you would find it.            | Task lets me evaluate whether the design decision to forego making 'decks' as a view in favor of searching for points/topics is intuitive, also checks whether user expectaitons of the search filter aligns with the implementation.          |
| Recall | You want to check your understanding of the correctness and runtime of mergesort. Walk me through how you would do that. | This is another centerpiece of the app, and I want to see if it's discoverable for a user that you can create prompts via selecting a group of points, or through selecting a subset of points in a topic. With a real user, I can discern how much frustration is felt at different parts of the process, and whether some steps I've gotten used to feel awkward.
| Schedule | The exam is coming up in a couple of weeks, so you'd like to make sure that you don't forget to practice quicksort while learning new material. Show me how you'd setup scheduling for it.| Having a user test this can give me a baseline for the inuitiveness of putting prompts on a schedule instead of points or topics.
| Sharing | Your friend Matthew is also studying for algorithms, and he's shared with you a prompt that's useful for studying. Show me how you'd find and use that prompt. | Lets me assess the learnability of making a snapshot of someone else's prompt.
| Flashcarding| To setup a post exam cramming tool, you want to write some answers to your points. Show me how you'd go about adding answers to points and reviewing those answers.| With a user, I can see from their facial expressions or statements whether the flashcarding seems useful, which is something I can't detect in my walkthrough.
